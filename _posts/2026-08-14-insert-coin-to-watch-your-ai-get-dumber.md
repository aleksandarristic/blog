> **PLAYER ONE READY**  
> Model: same. Version: same. Effort: same.  
> Intelligence: apparently a seasonal item.
> Now with verbal abuse of the agent.  
> Turn one line of code into a forty-minute argument with autocomplete.  
> Same frontier intelligence. New and exciting reasons to call it a fucking idiot.

Yesterday, the agent opened a repository, found the relevant code, understood the task, changed the right files, ran the tests, and explained what it had done like a competent adult who had, at some point in its life, held a job.

Today, the same agent — same product, same model name, same effort setting, same monthly bill — has spent twenty minutes repeatedly discovering the file I named in the first sentence, like a goldfish doing a lap of honor around the one castle in its bowl.

Welcome back, player. Your save file has been corrupted by somebody else's quarterly target. Continue?

There was no patch note saying **Reduced object permanence by 40%** or **Agent may now forget the task while actively performing it, the way a toddler forgets it is holding scissors**. The label on the machine did not change. The price did not change. Only the thing inside the box got dumber, like a hotel swapping your king bed for a cot while you were asleep and billing you for the suite.

Somewhere a product manager is looking at a GPU utilization graph, watching it dip below target. One might think that's actually the whole business model. Sell a genius in the demo, ship a paste-eater on Thursdays, and call the difference "variance."

Press any key to pretend you imagined it. Nobody's coming to explain it to you. There's no support ticket for "my colleague's IQ dropped forty points and finance says that's expected behavior." There's just you, the invoice, and a machine confidently renaming a variable it already renamed four times, cheerful as ever, dumb as a bag of hammers, still billing by the token.

GAME OVER. INSERT COIN.
